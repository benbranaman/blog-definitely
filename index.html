10/01/26

After what feels like nearly a year of waiting, today was my first official day of term at Cambridge. That being said, most of the excitement of the new school year is
still yet to come. Though I moved in a few days back, the orientation for the criminology program doesn’t actually begin until next week, and while my college,
Clare Hall, has several ceremonies that formally mark entry to the university (induction, matriculation, mutilation, defenestration, and more!) these too don’t
begin until the weekend.

So, with plenty of time on my hands, a lot of exploring has been in order. The first order of business has been getting the lay of the land of Cambridge’s downtown
area. Fortunately, there really only are like two main streets to deal with, so getting lost hasn’t been much of an issue thus far. From where I’m located west of
the center, I walk into the city crossing a bridge over the river Cam (you’ll never guess where the name Cambridge came from) before passing by a few of the
university’s oldest/richest colleges: Trinity and St. John’s. While Clare Hall is pretty informal and doesn’t - at least from what I can tell so far - have too many
rituals, some other colleges are a whole different animal. At Trinity at least, even walking on the grass of the grounds is strictly forbidden. The saying goes, at
least I’ve heard, that if you do they let you choose whether you’d rather be cremated or buried. That being said, on their grounds they do also have a descendant of
Isaac Newton’s apple tree (a sentence that doesn’t really feel real typing it out) so they have their reasons I suppose.

While the history of the city is omnipresent, it is also, somewhat surprisingly, very modern. And by modern, I in part mean that there’s a level of influence from
American fast food chains that I really didn’t think had crossed the pond or would be present in such a quaint setting. Bizarrely, it is here in England that I have
a nearby Shake Shack for the first time. Of course, there are also British alternatives to the American invasion, and a big part of my week has been trying to check
them off one by one. Yesterday I finally completed the trifecta of what fellow Clare Hall-ian Naim has referred to as the three good things of Britain* with a trip
to Wetherspoon’s, or Spoon’s if you’re in the know, which is a pub chain that in Cambridge is located just downstairs of the city’s independent cinema. Ours is also
a disco spoons which means they have DJ sets on the weekends, which, I have been informed, is a big deal. As this year is all about getting in the spirit of
empirical research I may have to go and make my own judgments in the near future.

More updates will be sure to come soon, especially as the year kicks off into high gear in the next few days. Also soon to come maybe will be my attempts at rowing as
I’m hoping to join one of Clare Hall’s teams in the near future! For now though, I need to try to remember how to code in HTML so enjoy this shorter entry for
now :)

*the other two being Nando’s and the Tesco meal deal of course
